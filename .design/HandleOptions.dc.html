Handle styling · pick one
Four treatments, same donut, realistic scale
All four share one rule: the visible grip is 6–8 px, the hit area is an invisible 18–22 px — today's 17 px painted squares are big because visual and hit size are the same thing. Splitting them is what fixes “huge”.
Option A — Dots. 8 px white dots on a 1 px hairline ring; rotate dot is amber. Quietest and smallest. Tradeoff: dots don't hint what they do until you grab one.
Option B — Ticks. Short strokes that cross the edge they edit: radial tick = push/pull that radius, arc tick = rotate. The shape teaches the gesture. Tradeoff: thin marks can vanish over busy telegraph art.
Option C — Hover-grow. Grips rest as 5 px muted dots and bloom to a haloed 12 px under the cursor. Near-invisible while reading the plan, obvious while aiming. Tradeoff: no resting affordance on touch, so touch keeps the full-size grips.
Option D — Edge lips. No selection ring and no separate grips: short bright arc segments on the shape's own edges are the handles (outer edge, inner edge, amber = rotate). Zero added geometry, most integrated look. Tradeoff: handle position varies per shape, so it's the most drawing work.
All options keep the amber-rotate convention and the live readout chip from the main proposal. C combines with A or B (they define the resting look; C is the hover behavior).
